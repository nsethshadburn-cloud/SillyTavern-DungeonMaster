# 🎲 Dungeon Master for SillyTavern

> [!WARNING]
> **BETA: experimental and unstable.** This is an early test build made for one person's setup. It can break, change without notice, behave strangely, or misbehave alongside other extensions. It spends real money on the Claude API (there's a budget cap, but costs are estimates). It creates group chats, lorebooks and files in your SillyTavern data. **Use it at your own risk, back up your SillyTavern data first, and please don't expect support.** No warranty of any kind.

Claude runs your campaign as the Dungeon Master. Your local model (Orion, or whatever you load) plays Judy and every NPC. Dice, events and bookkeeping are handled by code.

For how it works under the hood, see [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

---

## Installing

1. In SillyTavern, open **Extensions** (the stacked-blocks icon) → **Install extension**.
2. Paste `https://github.com/nsethshadburn-cloud/SillyTavern-DungeonMaster` and choose **Install just for me**.
3. Updating later: Extensions → **Manage extensions** → the update (⟳) button next to Dungeon Master.

Settings, character sheets and campaigns are stored per SillyTavern install, so a new machine starts fresh: set your DM voice and budget again and re-upload your persona sheet there.

## First-time setup (5 minutes)

1. **Reload SillyTavern** (F5). The extension loads automatically once it's installed for your SillyTavern user.
2. Open **Extensions** (the stacked-blocks icon) and find **🎲 Dungeon Master**. Check:
   - **DM profile:** "Auto" uses your saved **Claude** profile and key. That's fine.
   - **Dungeon Master models:** the **Main DM model** is Claude Sonnet 5.5. **Hybrid models** is on by default: every job has its own model dropdown (Setup, Story direction, Combat, Everyday play, Housekeeping, Dice). Recommended, and the default: Sonnet only for the one-off setup the story builds on (campaign design, pitches, the opening, your PDF sheet, party sheets, and maps when Claude plans every room); Haiku 5.5 for everything during play: rulings, roll results, travel, events, check-ins, companion tasks, new NPCs, questions, combat rounds, story direction (steering back to the plot, stuck scenes, deadlocks) and housekeeping. (v0.24 and v0.25.2 moved those jobs from Sonnet to Haiku in saved settings too, unless you had picked another model for them.) With **"Let cheaper models hand a big story moment up"** on, Haiku passes only the biggest moments (a major reveal, completing a plot beat, a new chapter) to the main model; fights, new places and everything else it handles itself. **Reset to recommended** puts it all back. Turn **Hybrid models** off and the main model does everything.
   - **Actors / Helper:** leave them on "current connection" unless you want a different server.
   - **Budget:** $20/month is set. The nightly soft cap ($0.65) makes the DM check in less often once it's reached.
   - **Voices:** pick a **DM voice** and press **Test DM voice**. Open **NPC voice pool** to switch voices on or off and fix any wrong genders.
3. **Your character sheet:** open the **Persona** panel and click the new **📜 scroll** button. Pick your 5e sheet in any form: a PDF (fillable or scanned), a photo or screenshot of a paper sheet, a Word document, or a text file. Claude converts it once (about 1-3¢; pictures cost a little more) and then it's locked.
4. **Thinking models (Gemma 4 / Orion):** nothing to do. In group chats SillyTavern starts each reply with "Name:", which makes these models skip thinking and garble words. In campaign chats the extension removes that one prefix automatically (other chats are untouched).
5. Optional: lower your **Response length** to about 2048 in the left sliders panel, so the story has room in the context.
6. **Recommended, saves the most money:** turn on Claude prompt caching. In SillyTavern's `config.yaml` (on the machine running SillyTavern), find the `claude:` section and set:
   ```yaml
   claude:
     enableSystemPromptCache: true
     cachingAtDepth: 2
   ```
   Restart SillyTavern, then tick **"I turned on Claude prompt caching"** in the extension settings. The DM's rules, character sheets, plot and notes are then reused from Claude's cache between turns at a tenth of the price or less, instead of being paid for in full every call. The cache lasts 5 minutes after each DM call; if you often go longer between DM turns, also set `extendedTTL: true` there and pick **1 hour** under **Cache lifetime** (writing to the cache costs a bit more, reading it stays cheap). These settings only affect Claude.

## Starting a campaign

Use the **magic wand menu → Start D&D campaign**, or type `/dmstart`.

1. Pick your persona and party members. Characters without 📜 get a sheet written by Claude.
2. Write your plot idea, or choose **Surprise me** → pick a tone and intensity → **Pitch me 3 ideas** → choose one.
3. Set the options (campaign feel, combat, rules, events) and click **Build campaign**.

A new group chat opens with the opening scene. Takes about 30–40 seconds and roughly 3–6¢.

## Playing

Just roleplay. Behind the scenes, each message goes through:

- **The helper** (your local model, under a second) reads your move.
- **Talking and roleplay** → your companions answer on the local model, playing freely. **Free.**
- **Risky things** (sneaking, lying, climbing, attacking), **travel**, **talking to someone new**, or a **random event** → the **DM (Claude)** steps in. Routine DM turns on Haiku cost a fraction of a cent; big moments on Sonnet about 1.5–5¢ (the low end with caching on).
- Every ~10 local turns the DM does a quick **check-in** to keep pacing on track.

**What the bot sees.** The DM's narration is for you only; the bot never reads it (that's what made it chase random details and drive the plot). Instead, when Claude handles a turn it gives each companion a short private **brief**: only what they need to know and want, never their words. Every reply also gets a short **scene note** (where you are, who's here, the goal, and a 3-4 sentence length target). You can read and edit all of this in **Settings → What the bot is told**, with **Reset to default** on each part and a **Preview** of exactly what the bot gets in the current chat.

**Combat.** Claude runs fights: it decides what companions and enemies do, rolls for them and narrates; companions only get a quick line. A **battle map** pops up (also in the wand menu, or `/dmmap`): chess-style squares (a1 is bottom-left), portraits, terrain, HP. Tap a square to move and an enemy to target, then **Add to message** and say what you do. On phones it's a sheet across the bottom of the screen.

**NPCs are real characters.** The first time an NPC speaks, the plugin makes a SillyTavern character card for them (tagged "D&D NPC: <campaign>") and adds them to the group chat, so their replies use your normal formatting and thinking, and you can swipe them. After 10 turns without speaking they leave the group; their card stays, so they come back the same when needed. Delete the tagged cards once a campaign is over if you like.

**Companions have a mind of their own.** Each companion has a private inner state that only the Dungeon Master keeps: their mood, a goal of their own (not the party's), how they feel about particular people, and a worry they haven't said out loud. It's set when the campaign is designed, updated only when something changes it, and every brief Judy gets is written from it, so she stays the same person from one turn to the next: she holds a grudge, keeps pushing the plan she wanted, warms up or cools off, and every few turns speaks up or does something nobody asked for. Your local model never sees it (that's on purpose: it only ever gets the brief). The DM Screen shows each companion's mind; **Fix mismatch** corrects it too.

**Companions know their story.** At the start, Claude writes each companion a private "what you know" summary (how they got here, what they're accused of, who they know), and it goes with every reply they give. The DM updates it now and then when something big changes. Older campaigns get one on the next DM turn.

**Inside a place, the location is the place.** Rooms are tracked by the map, so the DM can't make everyone "elsewhere" by naming a room, and outside a fight it can't move you or a free companion around the map: you walk, or the map sync walks companions from what they say. Only someone tied up or unconscious can be carried by the DM's positions.

**The DM speaks only when it should.** It posts only to describe the result of an action or roll, a new place or time skip, something new arriving, what a check reveals, or to ask for a roll. The rest (directing Judy and NPCs, state changes) happens silently. Dice cards always show. It never turns what you say into something you do, and when you keep refusing something, the other character changes approach instead of asking again.

**The DM is thinking.** While Claude works, a "The Dungeon Master is thinking…" bubble shows at the bottom of the chat.

**Textured maps.** Maps are painted, not flat squares: stone slabs with cracks and moss, wood grain, grass tufts, cobbles, water with ripples, glowing lava, plus furniture and items (chests, barrels, tables, braziers, altars, coffins, bones, webs, campfires and more). Every tile is drawn with its own variation, so nothing visibly repeats, and dark places like crypts and caves are lit by their torches and braziers. Each map has a theme (crypt, cave, tavern, forest, castle...). Hover or tap a square to see what's there.

**Maps outside fights.** In places where exact squares matter (a dungeon room, a trapped corridor, sneaking past guards) the DM can open a tile map, used sparingly; it closes when you leave. If a fight breaks out there, the room becomes the battle map.

**Whole places you explore.** When the story moves somewhere it will stay for a long time (the mansion you're held captive in, a dungeon, a castle), the map algorithm builds every room on every floor once, and the map is kept for the rest of the campaign. A big place is properly big: a big jail, castle, mansion or dungeon has 36–55 rooms over several floors (a small cottage can be a single room). Your local model (or Haiku) writes a short brief (what the place is, how big, where you start, and the few things the story needs there: the ledger in the hidden study, the warden with the keys); the code builds everything else from that brief and a seed, in seconds and nearly free. It asks you first for big places ("Build Blackmoor Manor?"), and a little map assembles itself in the chat while it works. (Settings → Map builder can switch back to Claude planning every room, about $0.15–0.30 a place.) Towns and villages are smaller maps: streets, roofed houses, hanging shop signs (an anvil for the smithy, a mug for the tavern), and each building's inside is built the first time you walk in. Nothing is ever built twice: going back to a place reopens its stored map with every door, trap and footprint as you left it.

- **Exploring:** tap a square and the party walks there (as far as you like outside a fight). Doors open as you go; locked ones need a key or a plan (tell the DM). Stairs take you to the next floor, and the floor picker shows the floors you've seen. The map follows you; scroll it to look around, and the 🎯 button follows you again.
- **Companions choose:** when you walk out of a room, your local model decides whether your companion comes along right now. They stand still until it has decided, then walk after you or stay. If they stay, they wait where they are (and can't hear you) until you come back into their room. Tap them for **Ask to follow** or **Ask to wait here**.
- **Speech bubbles:** when a companion or NPC speaks, a bubble pops up over their figure with what they said (the part in quotation marks), the words appearing one by one. With voices on it waits for their voice to start and keeps pace with it; with voices off (or if the voice fails) it appears as the line comes in. It stays for five seconds after they finish, then fades. Off in the settings.
- **Little animated figures:** everyone on the map is a small pixel figure standing on a colored ring (gold = you, green = companions, red = enemies, blue = others). They breathe and blink, walk when they move, swing their weapon when they attack, flash red when hit and lie down at 0 HP. Your persona and companions get their own figure, described once by Claude from their card and picture (about 1-2 cents each, kept for every campaign). Men and women are easy to tell apart: men get a heavier brow, a nose, a square jaw, broader shoulders and sometimes stubble, a mustache or a beard.
- **Hiding:** tap a wardrobe, barrel, chest, bed, table, bookshelf, statue... and choose Hide: you walk over and hide in it, under it or behind it (a short duck-in animation; then only your eyes peek out, or a faint crouch behind it). The DM asks for Stealth when it matters. Walk anywhere, or tap yourself and choose Come out, to leave. Companions who say they're hiding somewhere do the same.
- **Maps fit the place:** a kept-up place (a lived-in mansion, a working prison, a castle) is organized like a real building, with a main hallway on each floor and rooms off it in rows, and tidy (furniture, no bones and rubble). Only ruins and old dungeons are mazes full of mess and decay.
- **Telling a companion to do something:** "Judy, go check that crate" -- the local helper spots that it is a task. Judy walks to the crate on the map, and when what she finds is not known yet (searching, opening, scouting, listening, sneaking) the DM decides it, rolls for her and tells her the result before she answers, so she no longer makes things up. Simple things (wait here, follow me) stay local.
- **Buildings are two maps:** a house, mansion, tavern, temple, castle or prison is built as its grounds outside (the building with its roof and front door, a path, its gardens, courtyard or graveyard, trees and nature for the setting) and its inside. Walk through the front door to go in; walk out of it to come back outside. Outdoor rooms are never put inside, and garden hedges never get windows.
- **No surprise exits:** maps only have a way out where one really exists (a dungeon's entrance); the DM moves the story on to new places. Walking out through a real way out asks you first.
- **Lit homes have no fog:** a lit, lived-in place shows all its rooms (no black squares); dark or ruined places keep the fog of war.
- **Use furniture:** tap a chair, armchair, sofa, throne or pew to sit down; a bed, hay or a pet bed to lie down or sleep (sleeping tells the DM). Your figure sits, lies down or gets tucked into bed under a blanket; sleepers float little Z's. Tap yourself to get up; walking gets you up too. Companions who say they sit or lie down somewhere do it on the map.
- **Hurt and out cold:** at low HP figures stand hunched over, clutching their side, and drip a little blood; at 0 HP they fall unconscious (automatically, and the map won't let them walk) until healed. The DM marks anyone too hurt to walk ("can't walk"), and the map holds them there: someone has to carry them.
- **Wild places:** a jungle, forest, swamp, riverbank or camp is built as land: its spots (a camp, a waterfall pool, a ruined shrine, a ford) as sunny clearings joined by winding trails, a river (bridged where trails cross) or ponds, thickets of overlapping trees, great trees, palms, ferns, tall grass, reeds and lily pads. A new jungle theme. Water is painted as water: shallows to deep, soft rounded shores, foam, ripples. Grass has soft light and dark patches; trails are soft winding paths.
- **Towns:** every building takes a shape (an L, a front gable, a corner turret, a bay window, a wooden porch) in bright colours with red, terracotta, green, blue or purple roofs. A walled town gets curtain walls, round towers, gatehouses on its roads and its market square in the middle. Crowded towns shrink buildings instead of leaving any out; ponds turn up more often.
- **Buildings with real architecture:** from outside, a mansion has wings round a front court (U, E or H), a round entrance hall bulging out under a half-dome, bay windows or a columned portico, and round corner towers with cone roofs, each part under its own hipped roof. A castle has curtain walls with battlements, round corner towers, a gatehouse, the keep in the bailey and sometimes a moat with a bridge. Temples get transepts and a portico, wizards a round tower, cottages bay windows. Claude picks the look in its plan; otherwise it fits the kind of building.
- **Rebuild a map:** `/dmrebuild` throws away the map you're on (or `/dmrebuild Blackmoor Manor` for a named one), with its outside or inside, and Claude plans it again with the newest rules. It asks first: what happened on that map is lost.
- **Map tiers (settings):** Off (no maps at all: the DM is never told about them and the whole campaign stays in the chat), Sometimes (maps where needed or when you ask; the default) or Always (every new place gets a map: small for a short stop, big only where a lot of the story happens; big ones still ask first).
- **Companions are quiet while you explore:** while you're walking, searching or working a lock and not talking to them, companions mostly keep quiet; when they do speak it's one short line. They know only the rooms they've seen (plus the place, if their story says they know it) and are told never to name a room, passage or route that isn't on that list.
- **Companions are players, not puppets:** tapping anyone on the map (you too) just shows their name, health and what they're doing. To get Judy to do something, say it in the chat: she answers in character, and the helper reads her reply. If she agrees to follow she comes along, if she agrees to stay right by your side she won't wander, if she says she'll wait she stays. Otherwise, about one new room in three she may go and look at something that catches her eye on her own, roll her own Investigation (it can turn up a trap or something hidden) and tell you what she found, all on your local model (no Claude call).
- **Traps grab you:** stepping on a trap catches you, with an animation that fits it: a snare yanks you up by the foot and you dangle upside down, a net drops over you, a pit swallows you to the chest, darts stick, spikes shoot up, gas, flames, falling stone, steel jaws, or an alarm bell, all with a flash and a "!". You stay caught (and can't move) until your save succeeds; on a failure the DM decides what happens next and frees you when you get loose.
- **Places that make sense (common-sense rules):** every plan, whoever made it, goes through rules before a wall is drawn. A jail cell has exactly one door, a locked barred cell door onto its cell block, and no other way out (except, maybe, a hidden tunnel someone dug); if you start locked up, the cell keys are kept outside, where a jailer would have them. Vaults and armories have one way in and it's locked, with the key somewhere sensible, never behind its own door. Bedrooms, baths, closets, pantries, studies and offices open off one room; they're never corridors. Stairs come up in halls and working rooms, never in a bedroom, a bath or a cell. The pantry opens off the kitchen and the kitchen serves the dining room. A building's way out is an entrance, hall, shop floor or back door on the ground floor. A secret door leads somewhere worth hiding. Cellars, cells and crypts are dark; vaults and cellars have no windows; rooms are sized for what they are. Guards patrol their own floor and never start on top of you. After the walls are drawn, every room is checked: no gaps in walls, one door to a cell.
- **No two places alike:** sixteen kinds of place (jail, mansion, castle, temple, tavern, house, wizard's tower, library, bank, barracks, shop, smithy, dungeon, crypt, cave, ship), each with its own must-have and maybe rooms, layouts (a spine corridor, wings, a central hall, a maze for ruins), name pools ("Lady's Chamber", "The Ochre Parlor", "Chapel of Saint Hesk"), locks and keys, residents and guards, traps, loot and a couple of twists from that kind's list: a half-dug tunnel behind a loose stone in a cell, a sealed nursery, a priest hole behind the library, a burnt wing, a portrait cut from its frame, a smugglers' stash, a desecrated altar, a dead adventurer with notes. Towns, wild places and every town building's inside are built the same way.
- **Rooms are described when you walk in:** the first time you enter a room, your local model writes a sentence or two from what's really there (never people: those are on the map), the DM voice reads it, and the code puts one together if there's no helper.
- **People are on the map from the start:** the place's guards, residents and the story's people are placed when it's built. The DM can't conjure a guard right next to you: anyone new comes in through a door or stairs you can't see and walks to where they're going.
- **Maps are built around what you're there for:** the objective (the idol you're after, the person you came to rescue, the vault) is put deep inside: at least half way from where you start to the furthest room, as far as the place allows. Waking up in a cell? The prison's way out is put far from the cells (never on the cell block). Dungeons, crypts and caves run along a route from the way in to the far end, with side branches (dead ends, loot) and a locked gate part-way whose key is somewhere before it; traps sit along the route and something stands guard just before the prize. Big places spread over several floors. If the story says the thing is close (by the entrance), it stays close.
- **Pick locks yourself, no waiting on the DM:** tap a locked door (or cell door) on the map: **Pick the lock** walks you up to it and rolls d20 + your Sleight of Hand (or your Thieves' Tools, if your sheet has them and they're better) on an open dice card; beat the lock and the door swings open. If you carry its key, **Unlock it** just opens it. Every lock has its own difficulty, set by the kind of place (a cottage's latch is easy, a mansion's study harder, a jail's cells harder still, a bank vault hardest), what the door guards, and a little chance; the menu tells you what kind of lock it is. Only after two or three failures in a row at the same door (or a natural 1, when the pick snaps) is the DM called, to decide whether anyone within earshot heard. No Claude call otherwise.
- **The map stays where you're looking:** when the map updates (someone moves, a door opens, a turn ends, even while it's minimized), the view stays where you scrolled it instead of jumping back to the corner or onto you. While it's following you, it keeps you in the middle; scroll away and it stays put until you tap the follow button.
- **The map comes first in a new campaign:** if the story starts somewhere worth a map (a prison, a mansion, a dungeon, a town), you're asked to build it before anything else happens (unless maps are off). Companions start where your setup says: if Judy is locked in a cage elsewhere in the prison, she's in a cage room you haven't found yet, not next to you. Then the DM reads the map and writes the opening to match it (the room you're in, its doors, who is where).
- **Companions notice you come and go:** leave Judy somewhere and come back, and she may react (glad to see you, asking what you found in the rooms you went through) before falling in with you again. Walk into the room where she's caged and she can talk to you again: she may beg you to get her out and tell you what she knows about the lock or the keys. Walk away from someone who's staying and they may call something after you. Each time the companion decides whether they'd say anything at all (often they don't), all on your local model.
- **A held companion keeps her secret:** a companion caged somewhere you haven't found doesn't reveal her room on your map (her eyes aren't yours); you find her by exploring.
- **Companions taken away are somewhere real:** when the story takes Judy away inside the place you're in (caged somewhere in the dungeon, locked in a cell upstairs), she's put in an actual room: the one the DM names, or a cell, kennel or other room for a captive that you haven't explored yet, far from you, in a cage if the story says so. She only shows on the map once you can see her. Reach her and get her out, and she comes along again. (Before, she was just taken off the map.)
- **Items are never people:** a "tin of lamp oil" or a key the DM mentions never turns into a figure on the map (and any that did are cleared away when the map loads).
- **Conditions wear off:** the DM gives a duration ("poisoned (1 hour)", "frightened (until the end of the fight)", "stunned (1 round)"), or a sensible default applies: brief ones like frightened or stunned end with the fight, lingering ones like poisoned or groggy with a long rest. The code takes them off when they run out and shows it on a 📜 card. Being tied up, unconscious or caged lasts until something in the story ends it.
- **Rests are done by the code:** when the party rests, the DM just says so. A short rest gives back short-rest abilities (Second Wind, Channel Divinity, a warlock's pact slots...) and anyone hurt spends hit dice (rolled, plus CON). A long rest restores all HP, half your hit dice, every spell slot and ability, and ends what a rest ends.
- **Spell slots, hit dice and limited abilities are counted:** worked out from each sheet's class and level (full, half and pact casters). The DM spends them when someone casts or uses an ability, and with none left they can't. What's left shows in the DM's view.
- **Death saves:** at 0 HP you're dying, not just unconscious. Companions' death saves are rolled in the open each round (each turn outside a fight); you're asked to roll your own. A hit while down is a failed save (two for a critical), a natural 20 brings you back with 1 HP, three successes and you're stable, three failures and you're dead. Damage at least your whole HP past 0 kills outright. Healing ends it.
- **The map sync double-checks:** it only adds someone the story actually names or describes, never someone only heard (barking somewhere, footsteps above). "The hound" is the Guard Dog already there, not a second dog. Plans ("I'll go check") don't move anyone. It also keeps items in step: an item picked up off the floor is gone from the room, a hidden one found is known, something put down is left there.
- **Conditions stay put:** something like "groggy" or "poisoned" that wore off isn't put back a few turns later because older chat mentions it.
- **Which one do you mean?** Say "I smash the bookshelf" or "Judy, open the kennel" and the code works out exactly which thing on the map that is. One match: you (or Judy) walk up to it first and the DM is told that exact one. Several in the room (two bookshelves): the map opens with each one ringed and numbered; tap one, or pick it from the list under the map ("Never mind" backs out). Saying "the bookshelf" again later means the same one; "the other bookshelf" asks again; "the north door" and "the nearest chest" work too.
- **The map keeps up with the story:** after each turn, in the background, a model reads what was just said next to what the map shows and puts on the map what the story did: the dog that burst out of the kennel appears beside it, Judy walks over to the kennel, Judy ducks behind the crate she hid behind, a door that got kicked in is broken, someone who left is gone. The code checks every change against the real map first, party members walk there (never jump), and it shows on a 🗺️ Map card. It runs on your local helper (free); without one, or if you pick it in the settings, on Haiku. Off in the settings too.
- **People with a purpose:** anyone set on attacking the party chases you on the map, a step for each of yours, and when they reach someone the DM is called to start the fight. Someone frightened runs away from you; someone following tags along. The local model decides what a newcomer wants when it puts them on the map.
- **Walking is a turn too:** after a dozen squares of walking with no chat in between, the world takes a turn: fire spreads, patrols and hunters move, and the DM gets a look in when something happens or the world is due to act.
- **The DM knows what you can sense:** for everyone on the map it's told how far they are and whether you could see them, only hear them (if they're loud) or not sense them at all, so nobody smells a guard's lamp oil from across the map. A guard who rolled and missed you stays unaware: they don't drift toward you or come after you until something gives you away.
- **Being seen takes a roll:** whether a guard notices someone sneaking is decided by a Perception or Stealth roll in the chat, never by the DM's say-so, and the result sticks: the DM (and Judy) are told every turn who has spotted whom, so a guard who saw Judy keeps knowing it.
- **Only those with you fight:** when a fight breaks out, a companion caged or held somewhere else, away, on another floor or out of sight and reach isn't in it: no initiative, no turns, never pulled to your side. Free her or reach her mid-fight and the code adds her (a ⚔️ card shows her initiative).
- **Initiative first:** when a fight starts, nobody acts until you've rolled initiative. Then each round goes in order: those above you act, then it stops for you, then those below you.
- **Your turn is yours:** after your action is resolved, a popup asks whether you're done (you may still move: tap a square, or say where you go). When you end it, those after you act, then the next round comes back to you. Turn it off under *Turn prompts* in the settings.
- **React before a hit lands:** when an enemy hits you, a popup asks whether you react. Uncanny Dodge and the like halve the damage on the spot; anything else (a spell, a feature) goes to the DM to rule on, and the damage waits.
- **Rolls follow your choices:** the DM never asks you for a Deception or Stealth roll you didn't go for. When a guard spots you, it narrates what he does and stops: talking, running, fighting or giving up is your call.
- **The map says when to wait:** while the DM is working the map shows an hourglass banner, and taps on squares wait.
- **A "!" over whoever has spotted you:** a guard, keeper or resident who sees you where you shouldn't be (or out of your cell) gets a yellow "!" on the map for a couple of turns, and the DM is told to play out what he does. A fellow prisoner doesn't raise the alarm.
- **Guards keep order, they don't kill:** a guard who catches you threatens, orders you back and moves to grab you and walk you to your cell. He fights only if you do. If you give in, in your own words ("fine", "I'll go", hands up), the code walks you to your cell and locks the door: a 🔒 card says so. The DM can't do that from silence or a threat.
- **Hits always hurt:** an attack that hits always takes HP off: if the DM leaves out who it's at, the target's AC or the damage dice, the code fills them in (from the attack's own words, the attacker's stats, or a plain 1d6).
- **Bodies stay a while:** someone who dies stays where they fell (lying in their blood) for three turns, then they're cleared away. You can walk over them.
- **Judy knows who's who:** companions are told plainly who's an enemy (anyone hostile, everyone in the fight against you), who's friendly or wary, who's dead, and who has spotted the party.
- **Traps where someone would set them:** a new map puts its traps just inside doorways, in front of chests, altars and thrones, partway down hallways and on the trails into a clearing, and only now and then somewhere random.
- **The bookkeeper:** after each turn, in the background, your local helper reads the DM's narration (only that: what companions and NPCs say isn't evidence) next to the party's tracked HP, conditions and items, and fills in what the story says happened but nobody wrote down: Judy passed out or can't walk, the key changed hands, the potion got used. The code checks it first (known conditions only, party members the narration actually names, nothing added twice or removed that isn't there) and it shows up on a 📜 Bookkeeping card. Enemies and people on the map are the DM's. Turn it off in the settings.
- **Bookkeeping:** the DM is told to put every change its story makes (HP, conditions like unconscious or can't walk, items gained, lost or handed over, gold) on the 📜 card the same turn. Items are tracked by who carries them.
- **Damage from saves is applied:** when a companion (or you) fails a save against a trap, a Fireball or poison, the code takes the HP off right away (half on a success when the effect says so) and shows it on the dice card. Before, only attack damage was applied, so a failed trap save could leave HP untouched.
- **Fire:** set things alight (a torch in the hay, a thrown lantern, Fireball) and the map burns for real. Every square has a burn rating: wooden floors, timber walls, doors, cloth, hay, books and furniture burn; stone, earth and water don't. Each turn (a chat turn, or a long walk) fire spreads to burnable squares next to it, flares up, burns down, and leaves charred floors and walls, burnt-out doorways and ash where furniture stood. A tavern can go up entirely. It lights up the dark, flickers, throws embers and smoke, and anyone standing in it takes fire damage every turn (applied for you, the DM narrates it). Companions walk around it.
- **Spells on the map:** fog, darkness, ice, webs, poison clouds, acid, holy light and water cover their area for their duration; lightning and thunder flash once. The DM is taught which spell is which (Fireball = fire with a 20 ft radius, Fog Cloud = fog...).
- **Ambience (toggle):** rain, snow, dust motes, ash, mist, falling leaves, fireflies, blowing sand, glints on water, flickering torches, bubbling lava. Picked from the kind of place (or the DM sets it when the weather changes), kept faint. Turn it off with the cloud button on the map or in the settings; fire and spells always show.
- **The DM knows who is hiding, live:** every DM call lists where you and your companions stand and whether each of you is hiding (and in, under or behind what), read straight off the map. Walk out of the barrel and the next call says you are out in the open, whatever was said before. When the DM narrates someone hiding or coming out, it updates the map too.
- **Companions find their own way:** when Judy follows you she isn't stepping in your footprints any more: every step she takes her own shortest way to a free square next to you (more room when it's crowded), so she cuts corners and walks beside you. Her own way can lead her onto a hidden trap you missed.
- **Warn your companions:** tell Judy about a trap ("Judy, watch out for the tripwire by the door") and she knows about it: she walks around it when there's another way, and only crosses it when there isn't. She also knows the ones she found herself, the ones she saw go off, and ones set in plain sight. Traps you found but didn't mention, she doesn't know about.
- **Traps the DM sets are real:** ask the DM to trap a room and the traps it sets go off when anyone steps on them (hidden ones too, until a search finds them). Trap tiles the DM put down before this version become real traps the next time the place loads.
- **Traps catch everyone:** a companion who walks onto a hidden trap (going to search something, going to hide) sets it off too, stops there, and the DM rolls their save. A sprung pit stays an open pit: walk into it again and you fall again. Walks take the shortest way, so crossing a trap you already know about sets it off too.
- **Run into the dark:** tap a black (unexplored) square and you go: the walk follows the real way there, with no warning about what's in it. A wall or locked door in the dark stops you right next to it; a trap on the way springs.
- **Hidden traps:** traps stay hidden until someone searches the room (a Perception or Investigation roll that beats the trap; tap yourself and choose Search for traps) or steps on one. Pressure plates, tripwires, pits, glyphs, needles, blades... Decay hazards like rotten boards only appear in ruins.
- **More NPC figures:** cooks, smiths, jailers, farmers, laborers, sailors, miners, scholars, bards, healers, beggars, elders, executioners and plain townsfolk join the library. NPCs never get their own drawn figure: only you and your companions do.
- **Jails look like jails:** cells sit in rows along a cell-block hallway, behind iron bars with barred cell doors (locked until someone deals with them). You can see through bars, so from your cell you see the hallway and the cells across it. If the story has you locked up, you start in your cell, together.
- **Companions act on what they say:** a companion who says they'll go somewhere ("I'll hide in the hay", "I'll check the next room") walks there on the map and stays. Companions also get a note about the room they're in on the map (its description, what's in it, the ways out), so they talk about what's really there.
- **Tied up, cuffed, caged:** restraints show on the map. Tied up: rope around the chest, arms and ankles, and the figure squirms. Cuffed: hands together in front in iron cuffs. Caged: iron bars around them. Tied up, grappled, shackled or caged, you can't move yourself (tap your own figure for ways to get free: struggle, slip free, pick the lock), but others can still drag or carry you. Cuffs bind the hands only: you can still walk. Works for companions and NPCs too.
- **Enemies are the people on the map:** when a fight starts, "the watchman" is the turnkey already standing there: he fights from his square and stays the same person after. Someone who falls stays where he fell.
- **Fights limit movement:** in a fight you move up to your speed each round (30 ft = 6 squares; the squares you can reach light up), one move per turn, not while the DM is working. If the DM rolls an attack without starting the fight, the fight starts by itself. Outside fights you walk freely.
- **Map figures window** (wand menu → *Map figures*, the *Map figures* button on the DM screen, or `/dmfigures`): watch your persona's and companions' figures animate (idle, walk, attack, hurt, down) and change them: type what you want ("give her a red cloak and a bow", "he's a fox, not a wolf") and press *Redraw*. Your instructions are kept for later redraws; *Back to the card* drops them. From the chat: `/dmfigure Judy give her a red cloak`. NPCs, guards and monsters (guards, nobles, servants, cultists, elves, dwarves, orcs, goblins, beastfolk, skeletons, zombies, ghosts, wolves, hounds, rats, spiders, horses, bears...) come from a built-in library in different colors. Turn them off in the settings to get round portraits back.
- **Furniture and windows:** tap a piece of furniture for **Search, Smash, Push**. Smashing takes a roll; on a success it becomes broken pieces you can walk over. Light things (chairs, crates, barrels) shove one square at a tap; heavy ones (bookshelves, wardrobes, beds) take a roll. Shove something into a doorway and the door is barricaded. Tap a window to **break** it (it's loud) and then **climb out**: out of a town building you land on the street beside it. Barred windows can only be pried at (a hard roll).
- **People on the map:** guards (orange ring: they keep order, they aren't enemies), enemies (red) and everyone else who belongs there (blue ring: a cook, a prisoner, the shopkeeper inside a shop). A jail is staffed from the start: a turnkey on every cell block, someone on the door. Tap someone for **Talk, Look, Attack, Sneak past, Steal**.
- **Who may be where:** every room is public, staff only, private or a cell, and you have a standing in each place: a prisoner may be in their own cell, an intruder nowhere, a guest in the public rooms (a tavern's taproom, a shop floor, a host's hall), a resident everywhere. Step where you're not allowed and the map shows a pulsing **⚠ TRESPASSING** (or **ESCAPED**, out of your cell) badge. Public buildings hang **No entry** signs by the doors into the back. A guard or resident who sees you there calls the DM in, who plays them as who they are: a guard challenges you or raises the alarm, a cook shouts. Seen where you're allowed, they just note you and may say a vague line in their bubble (a guard: "Hope you're not up to trouble"; anyone, if you're badly hurt: "You don't look so good"), with no Claude call. The DM widens or narrows your standing when the story does: a guard waves you through, the host invites you in, you're caught and locked up.
- **People keep to their place:** a posted guard paces his room a square at a time, patrols walk their rounds slowly, residents potter about and now and then go next door. In the dark, guards carry lanterns: they see three squares around them, and you see their light coming. Otherwise everyone only notices within two squares in the dark, and within eight in the light. The place's own people carry its keys: a turnkey walks through the locked cell-block door and locks it behind him; a monster can't.
- **The clock:** the map's corner shows the time of day, the hour, the day and the weather (☀️ 14:20 day 2 🌧️). Time passes by itself, about ten minutes a turn (a setting), a minute a round in a fight, an hour for a short rest and the night for a long one; the DM can't decide it's morning, it can only skip ahead when the story does ("you sleep till dawn", "you wait for dusk"), and a 🕰️ card shows the skip. The weather drifts on its own as the hours pass, by the climate of where you are, and the DM knows it: rain and fog hide you and wash out tracks, a storm drowns sound, snow shows tracks. Outdoors the map shows it (rain, snow, mist), fog cuts everyone's sight to four squares, and a 🌦️ card tells you when it turns.
- **Night is real:** from evening on the outdoors goes dark, a house's rooms are dark except where someone is awake or a light burns, and at night residents sleep (on their beds), shops and homes in town lock up (taverns, inns, guardhouses and jails stay open), and night guards carry lanterns. The map dims and says 🌙. A torch or lantern in your pack lights three squares around you. Morning wakes everyone.
- **Asleep, knocked out, tied up:** someone the DM puts to sleep, knocks out or ties up (or a fight leaves at 0 HP without killing) notices nothing and goes nowhere until they're woken or freed; the dead stay dead, and a body stays where it fell until you've been out of sight of it a while and someone is left to clear it. A Stealth roll that beats a guard keeps you unnoticed by him until you're right beside each other. After a fight, a guard who yielded is a guard again, not an enemy.
- **Companions know who is in the room:** the note each companion gets names everyone in the room with them from the map (a turnkey four squares off, asleep; you), lists only the NPCs actually in reach as present, and carries the hour and the weather. The bookkeeper reads the narration for the people on the map too: a guard the story knocks out or ties up is out of it on the map until the story wakes or frees him.
- **Deleting a turn undoes all of it:** HP, items, the clock, the map, the notes, the timeline, and what your companions learned or felt in it.
- **What they say is said:** a vague line from someone who notices you (a turnkey to a prisoner in her cell: "Quiet in there."; a shopkeeper by day: "Good day.") shows in their bubble and in the chat as their own line, so your companions and the DM hear it too. Nobody greets you mid-conversation, or at an unconscious friend. Pick a lock in a guard's sight and the DM hears of it; start a fight and whoever is within earshot may come.
- **Fog of war:** you only see the room you're standing in (or the corridor). The next room stays dark until you step inside, even if it's lit, and walls and closed doors block your view. Lit rooms show whole; dark ones only around you and near torches. Places you've been stay on the map, dimmed.
- **Fights inside a place** use the floor as the battle map, fog and all. Everyone moves at their speed (30 ft = 6 squares; rubble and shallow water cost double): the map only lets you pick squares you can reach this round, and Claude's moves for your companions are held to the same limit (double when they Dash).
- **Traps and guards:** a hidden trap on your path is either spotted (passive Perception) or sprung, and the DM steps in. Guards patrol on their own, walking their rounds a few squares a turn (never jumping from room to room), and the DM is called when one comes into view.
- **Commands:** `/dmbuild big Blackmoor Manor` or `/dmbuild small Hollowbrook village` builds one yourself.

**NPCs stay around.** NPCs stay in the group chat until they die or go 100 turns without being spoken to or speaking; they can always be added back.

**Every roll is shown.** Dice cards always say who rolled, for what, and the number. If a companion sneaks or steals near you, they roll against your passive Perception; you're never asked to roll in your sleep.

**Judy's own attempts get rulings.** When Judy tries something risky on her own (slipping a key, sneaking off, lying to a guard), the helper notices and the DM rules on it with a roll for her, just like your actions.

**After updating:** if the plugin notices your browser is still running an older cached copy, it pops up a warning to press Ctrl+Shift+R.

**Companions act like players.** In turns the DM is already handling, it can have Judy use her own abilities (Misty Step, Help, her own Perception), and her rolls show as dice cards. When a check applies to everyone (all climbing, all sneaking), the DM rolls for her too, and each of you succeeds or fails on your own roll.

**Levels.** Ask the DM out of character, e.g. `((level Judy to 5))`, or type `/dmlevel Judy 5`. Claude rewrites her sheet for that level by 5e rules and her HP follows.

**Fix a mismatch.** Deleted or edited messages? DM Screen → **Fix mismatch** (or the wand menu, or `/dmresync`). Claude re-reads the chat as it is now and corrects what it has logged: HP, items, location, plot beats, notes, story so far, NPCs met, what companions know.

**Companions talk when it makes sense.** Judy (and any other companion) only replies when she's with you and would naturally react; she can stay quiet. If Amber splits off, the DM marks her as away (and where she is), she can't hear or speak until you regroup, and your scenes alone are answered by the DM instead.

**The note is sized to your context.** Settings → "Max tokens the bot's note may add" (600 by default) caps what the plugin adds to a local prompt, and it never takes more than a sixth of the context your model has left after its response. When the note has to shrink, the repeated-phrases line goes first, then what the companion knows, the room, the scene card; the DM's brief and "just happened" always arrive whole.

**How the bot's note is added.** The note goes inside your latest message for that one reply (your saved chat isn't changed), so the prompt keeps exactly the turns your own format and SillyTavern build. Thinking models like Gemma 4 need their `<|think|>` setup to stay the only system turn; an extra system message from the plugin right before the reply made them skip thinking. The old way is still available in settings ("How the note is added").

**The world doesn't wait.** Every few turns the DM steps in on its own to move the scene a step: guards keep processing you, a march goes on, time passes, someone shows up. Its normal pace is every 5 turns (Settings → "World pace"); it goes faster, every turn or two, when people are actively doing something to you, and slower in genuinely quiet moments. While you're really talking (not just "ok"), the clock runs at half speed so conversations get room to breathe. It works around your conversation instead of cutting it off.

**Pacing.** The DM keeps the story moving: every narration changes something, boring stretches (long walks, waits) get skipped to the next interesting moment, and when you arrive somewhere new it describes the place (name, look, sounds, who's around). Real conversation is never rushed, even while traveling. If your last few messages are short filler ("ok", "lets go", "*nods*") with no questions or plans, it treats the scene as stalled and moves things along.

**Staying on the plot.** Wander off as much as you like. The longer you go without reaching the next plot beat, the harder the world pushes back, one step at a time: hints and rumors, then pursuers, then direct threats, and finally a fair but overwhelming turn of events that puts the story back on track. It holds off while you're in a real conversation, and the moment you reach the beat's goal (even in a way the plot didn't plan) the pressure resets. The DM Screen shows the current pressure and the next beat.

**Random events** only happen when they fit: when the dice say "maybe", Claude looks at a few candidates and the situation, and picks one, invents a better-fitting one, or skips it.

**Surprises.** Settings → **Surprises**: toggles for traps, hazards, puzzles and locks, secret doors, hidden treasure and ambushes, plus how often.

**Voices.** In D&D chats the plugin voices everyone (DM, NPCs, and Judy with the voice from SillyTavern's TTS panel) one at a time, in chat order. SillyTavern's own TTS is switched off while a D&D chat is open and back on when you leave.

|---|---|
| Roll when the DM asks | Use SillyTavern's dice (they're read automatically, including advantage and damage dice), or roll real dice and type the number (`14`, `nat 20`, `rolled 12 total 17`) |
| Ask the DM something out of character | Start with `((`… or `DM,`… e.g. `((how far is the city?))` or type `/dm how far is the city?` |

**The status bar** above the chat box shows each step live (helper → DM → who's responding) with time and estimated cost. Click the arrow to expand it, and the ✕ to hide it.

**Dice cards** (🎲) show every roll and, in plain words, what happened, e.g. `🎲 Bandit 1 · Crossbow at Judy: 1d20 (18) + 4 = 22 ✔ vs AC 15 · 5 damage → The bolt grazes Judy's arm.` They're hidden from the local model; the character it affects gets the result in their brief.

## Deleting a campaign

Delete the campaign's chat and you'll be asked whether to delete the campaign too: its story, notes and plot files, every map it built, and the NPC character cards made for it.

## Deleting, editing and swiping messages

- **Deleting the DM's last turn undoes it:** delete a turn's DM narration (or every message a turn wrote) and HP, conditions, items, gold, the fight, who's away, the map you're on (people, doors, traps, fire) and any roll the DM was waiting for go back to how they were before that turn. It works for the latest turns, newest first (up to six back, this session); deleting an older turn while newer ones stand leaves the state alone and tells you. Ticked plot beats, NPCs met, DM notes and the logs don't rewind: fix those in the **DM Screen** (Files) or tell the DM: `((I deleted that last scene, ignore it))`.
- **Editing messages** works like normal SillyTavern: the DM reads the chat as it is now on its next turn.
- **Deleting a 🎲 roll request** doesn't cancel it. Use **DM Screen → Cancel** next to "Waiting for your roll".
- **Swiping a party member's reply** (e.g. Judy) works: she gets the same brief from the DM as the first time (or none, if it was free roleplay).
- **Don't swipe Dungeon Master or NPC messages.** They aren't characters, so SillyTavern can't regenerate them. Delete the message and use **DM: step in now** instead.

## Where your stuff lives

- **Campaign files:** `data/<your-user>/user/files/dm_<id>_*` (bible, plot, notes, story, world state, NPCs, event/roll logs, costs). Plain text and JSON; you can read and edit them.
- **Character sheets:** `data/<your-user>/user/files/dmsheet_*.md`
- **Random event deck:** `extensions/SillyTavern-DungeonMaster/data/events.json` (301 events). Edit it freely: add events, set `weight: 0` to disable one, use `requires: ["night"]` for night-only events.
- **Memories:** each campaign gets a "DnD Memories - …" lorebook for MemoryBooks, and the DM reads it.

## Good to know

- **Costs are estimates.** SillyTavern doesn't pass Claude's real token counts back to extensions. Your Anthropic console shows the true spend. In testing, a DM turn was about 2¢ and a campaign build about 3–5¢.
- **Your settings are never changed** apart from SillyTavern's TTS on/off switch, which the plugin turns off in D&D chats (so voices don't overlap) and back on when you leave. The extension uses whatever instruct, system prompt and samplers you have selected.
- **NPCs get voices** from the NPC voice pool, matched by gender, and keep them for the whole campaign.
- **Pick "Current connection" for Actors** unless you really need a different server: a connection profile there switches your whole setup (including formatting) for every companion reply.
- **Claude won't write explicit sexual content.** The DM will cut away or steer if a scene heads there.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Claude call failed" | Check the SillyTavern server window for the real error (key, model name, network). |
| A character's reply hangs | Look for a popup from another extension waiting for an answer. |
| Wrong pronouns or voice for an NPC | Edit their `gender` / `voice` in `dm_<id>_npcs.json`. Your Orion prompt says "she" a lot, so NPC prompts state pronouns explicitly. |
| Nothing happens after I send | The chat must be a campaign chat (made by the wizard) and not paused. Check the DM Screen. |
| Budget reached | The party keeps playing locally. Raise the monthly cap in settings to bring the DM back. |

Run the logic tests (for developers): `node --test tests/core.test.mjs` in this folder.
